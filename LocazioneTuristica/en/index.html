The name

Why the colour is called magenta

In 1859, at the Battle of Magenta, Franco-Piedmontese forces defeated Austria. Soon after, a vivid aniline dye in France was named magenta after that victory — so the Lombard town also named a colour used worldwide.

Milan without the chaos

Trains to Garibaldi and Centrale

From Magenta station, rail links reach Porta Garibaldi and Milano Centrale, then metro and downtown. In peak hours trains are often about every 15 minutes (line and timetable depending): enjoy the city by day, sleep in a real apartment at night.

Ideal if you want Duomo, Navigli, museums or meetings without downtown hotel prices.

Magenta memory

The Gamba de Legn

From the late 1800s to 1957, the steam tram nicknamed Gamba de Legn linked Magenta (and Castano) to Milan — a symbol of commuters, markets and wartime evacuations. Historic Milan terminus: Piazzale Baracca then Corso Vercelli. Last run: 31 August 1957; a locomotive is now at the Science and Technology Museum.

That line already told Magenta’s story: a gateway to Milan. Today modern trains play that role toward Garibaldi, Centrale and the city centre — while motorway and Malpensa open Magenta to international fairs.

Nature

Ticino Park

A few minutes from Magenta begins the Lombardy Ticino Valley Nature Park: river, woods, cycle paths and Navigli landscapes. A green break between fair days and the city.
